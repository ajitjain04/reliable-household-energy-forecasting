1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & RESEARCH MANDATE
================================================================================
This report synthesizes the empirical evidence, statistical tests, explainability
analyses, and uncertainty quantification results from Steps 4 through 9 of the
research project: "Reliable Multimodal Household Energy Forecasting Under Degraded
Weather Information."

The core research question investigates whether fusing auxiliary weather variables
(temperature, apparent temperature, relative humidity, atmospheric pressure, wind
speed, wind sin/cos components) with past household electricity consumption improves
forecasting accuracy, and whether multimodal neural architectures maintain reliable,
calibrated predictions when weather or electricity sensors suffer operational
degradation (sensor missingness and measurement noise).

The experimental investigation evaluated four distinct neural architectures:
  1. Energy-Only LSTM (Unimodal autoregressive baseline, 51,992 parameters)
  2. Early Fusion LSTM (Joint concatenation input encoder, 53,784 parameters)
  3. Late Fusion LSTM (Independent unimodal feature extractors, 112,216 parameters)
  4. Hybrid Fusion LSTM (Cross-modal projection with shared representation, 145,496 parameters)

All models were evaluated under identical frozen conditions:
  - Temporal input context: 168 hours (7 days) of hourly historical observations
  - Forecast horizon: 24 hours ahead (multi-step autoregressive rollout)
  - Withheld evaluation set: 587 rolling test windows (14,088 forecast points)
  - Controlled degradation regimes:
      * 30% MCAR Missingness: Mean-value masking (zero in standardized space)
      * 20% Additive Gaussian Noise: Zero-mean AWGN (eta = 0.20 in standardized space)
      * 2x2 Factorial Matrix: Clean (N-N), Weather-Degraded (N-A), Electricity-
        Degraded (A-N), and Joint-Degraded (A-A)
  - Inferential Battery: 78 paired statistical tests (Wilcoxon Signed-Rank,
    Holm-Bonferroni correction, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR, HLN Diebold-Mariano)
  - Explainable AI: Integrated Gradients across 587 windows (m = 20 steps)
  - Uncertainty Quantification: Horizon-Wise Absolute-Residual Split Conformal
    Prediction (N_cal = 587, alpha = 0.10, nominal coverage = 90.0%)

7. CROSS-STEP SYNTHESIS & EVIDENCE CONNECTIONS
================================================================================
Synthesizing Steps 4 through 9 reveals three interconnected research insights:

Insight 1: Accuracy and Robustness Across Fusion Architectures
  - Early Fusion achieved the lowest clean-data point forecasting error (71.64 W MAE)
    and the lowest prediction interval score (360.65 Winkler score).
  - Late Fusion and Hybrid Fusion showed lower relative error increases under
    sensor missingness (Hybrid increased by +2.95% relative MAE under A-A vs
    +6.64% for Early Fusion) and maintained higher empirical interval coverage (Late
    Fusion retained 88.42% PICP under A-A).
  - In absolute terms, Early Fusion's error under dual missingness (76.39 W MAE)
    remained lower than Hybrid Fusion's clean error (82.19 W MAE).

Insight 2: Modality Asymmetry and Feature Attribution Density
  - The empirical finding that electricity missingness causes larger error increases
    than weather missingness corresponds to the Step 8 Integrated Gradients analysis:
    electricity accounts for 76.2% to 88.4% of single-channel attribution density.
  - In household load forecasting, weather variables provide secondary contextual
    signals, whereas past consumption reflects direct occupant behavioral routines.

Insight 3: Noise Invariance vs Zero-Imputation Effects
  - 20% additive Gaussian noise produced small forecasting changes (<= 0.10 W MAE
    increase), non-significant differences in multimodal models (p_holm = 1.0),
    minor attribution shift (<= 0.08% shift), and stable conformal coverage.
  - In contrast, 30% MCAR zero-masking caused substantial degradation because
    zero-imputation in standardized space replaces missing values with the training mean
    (307.17 W), shifting predictions during periods of deviation from the mean.

8. METHODOLOGICAL LIMITATIONS & THREATS TO VALIDITY
================================================================================
1. Single Household Temporal Scope:
   All evaluations were performed on 587 rolling windows from a single residential
   meter. While providing temporal granularity over multi-season transitions,
   findings characterize this specific dwelling and cannot be generalized
   unconditionally across different building archetypes, microclimates, or solar prosumers.
2. Sliding Window Autocorrelation:
   Consecutive 24-hour forecast windows overlap by 23 hours in the sliding-window
   protocol. While paired testing on identical windows controls for shared ambient
   conditions and the HLN-DM test applies Newey-West HAC covariance corrections, the
   effective degrees of freedom is lower than N = 587 independent trials.
3. Fixed Degradation Protocols:
   The study strictly examined 30% MCAR missingness (mean-imputed) and 20% AWGN noise.
   Structural multi-hour contiguous outages, sensor drift, delayed telemetry, or
   extreme noise levels were not evaluated.
4. Marginal vs Conditional Conformal Coverage:
   Split conformal prediction provides finite-sample marginal coverage guarantees
   under exchangeability. It does not provide formal conditional guarantees for
   specific hours or peak load regimes, and coverage naturally erodes when test
   inputs suffer distribution shift.
5. Local Gradient Explainability:
   Integrated Gradients measures model sensitivity relative to a mean baseline;
   it establishes computational reliance within the network, not physical causality.

9. EVIDENCE-BASED RESEARCH CONCLUSIONS
================================================================================
1. Multimodal Early Fusion Yielded the Lowest Error in This Study:
   Early Fusion achieved the lowest forecast error under clean operation (71.64 W MAE,
   p < 1e-16), retained the lowest error under dual sensor degradation (76.39 W MAE),
   and produced the lowest prediction interval score (360.65 Winkler score).
2. Decoupled Architectural Complexity Did Not Improve Forecast Accuracy:
   Late Fusion (112,216 parameters) and Hybrid Fusion (145,496 parameters) substantially
   increased model capacity and training runtime compared to Early Fusion (53,784
   parameters) and Energy-Only (51,992 parameters), but did not achieve lower test
   forecasting error than Early Fusion under nominal clean conditions or under sensor degradation.
3. Household Load Forecasting is Inherently Asymmetric:
   Energy history is the dominant operational signal. Auxiliary weather data provides
   secondary contextual gains, but weather inputs cannot substitute when
   electricity history is missing.
4. Recurrent Architectures Handled 20% AWGN Noise but Degraded Under 30% Missingness:
   The tested recurrent architectures showed very little sensitivity to 20% AWGN noise,
   but suffered measurable performance drops under 30% MCAR missingness with mean imputation.
5. Fixed Conformal Intervals Maintained Width but Lost Coverage Under Missingness:
   Fixed clean-calibrated conformal intervals maintain constant width across conditions,
   which resulted in lower empirical coverage when test inputs suffered missingness.
