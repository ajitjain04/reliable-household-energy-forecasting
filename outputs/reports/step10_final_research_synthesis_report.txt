1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & RESEARCH MANDATE
================================================================================
This report synthesizes the empirical evidence, statistical tests, explainability
analyses, and uncertainty quantification results from Steps 4 through 9 of the
research project: "Reliable Multimodal Household Energy Forecasting Under Degraded
Weather Information."

The core research question investigates whether fusing auxiliary weather variables
(temperature, apparent temperature, relative humidity, atmospheric pressure, wind
speed, wind sin/cos components) with past household electricity consumption improves
forecasting accuracy, and whether multimodal neural architectures maintain reliable,
calibrated predictions when weather or electricity sensors suffer operational
degradation (sensor missingness and measurement noise).

The experimental investigation evaluated four distinct neural architectures:
  1. Energy-Only LSTM (Unimodal autoregressive baseline, 51,992 parameters)
  2. Early Fusion LSTM (Joint concatenation input encoder, 68,760 parameters)
  3. Late Fusion LSTM (Independent unimodal feature extractors, 104,088 parameters)
  4. Hybrid Fusion LSTM (Cross-modal projection with shared representation, 107,672 parameters)

All models were evaluated under identical frozen conditions:
  - Temporal input context: 168 hours (7 days) of hourly historical observations
  - Forecast horizon: 24 hours ahead (multi-step autoregressive rollout)
  - Withheld evaluation set: 587 rolling test windows (14,088 forecast points)
  - Controlled degradation regimes:
      * 30% MCAR Missingness: Mean-value masking (zero in standardized space)
      * 20% Additive Gaussian Noise: Zero-mean AWGN (eta = 0.20 in standardized space)
      * 2x2 Factorial Matrix: Clean (N-N), Weather-Degraded (N-A), Electricity-
        Degraded (A-N), and Joint-Degraded (A-A)
  - Inferential Battery: 78 paired statistical tests (Wilcoxon Signed-Rank,
    Holm-Bonferroni correction, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR, HLN Diebold-Mariano)
  - Explainable AI: Integrated Gradients across 587 windows (m = 20 steps)
  - Uncertainty Quantification: Horizon-Wise Absolute-Residual Split Conformal
    Prediction (N_cal = 587, alpha = 0.10, nominal coverage = 90.0%)

Critical Robustness Observations:
1. Marked Modality Sensitivity Asymmetry:
   Across all architectures, electricity history missingness caused severe performance
   degradation (error increases of +1.89 W to +9.13 W), whereas weather missingness
   caused negligible degradation (+0.17 W to +0.65 W). Weather degradation alone
   degraded Early Fusion MAE by only +0.24% (+0.17 W).
2. Missingness vs Noise Impact Divergence:
   30% MCAR missingness caused up to two orders of magnitude greater error degradation
   than 20% AWGN noise. For Early Fusion under dual degradation (A-A), missingness
   increased MAE by +4.76 W (+6.64%), while noise increased MAE by only +0.09 W (+0.13%).
   The degradation ratio of missingness over noise reached 51.7x for Early Fusion and
   74.3x for Hybrid Fusion.
3. Decoupling of Clean Accuracy and Relative Robustness:
   Hybrid Fusion demonstrated the smallest relative degradation under dual missingness
   (+2.95% relative MAE increase vs +6.64% for Early Fusion and +11.72% for Energy-Only).
   However, because Early Fusion started from a far superior clean baseline (71.64 W
   vs 82.19 W), Early Fusion's degraded performance under dual missingness (76.39 W)
   remained significantly superior to Hybrid Fusion's clean performance (82.19 W).
4. Strict Verification of Energy-Only Invariance Controls:
   The Energy-Only baseline ignores weather features by construction. As required by
   protocol integrity, its metrics under Weather Missingness (N-A) were identical to
   Clean N-N (diff <= 2.5e-5 W), and under Joint Missingness (A-A) were identical to
   Electricity-Only Missingness (A-N, diff = 0.0 W). The same control identities held
   under Noise (N-A == N-N, A-A == A-N).

Principal Explainability Findings:
1. Dimensionality Disparity vs Per-Channel Density:
   While raw attribution share indicates that weather channels capture 46.7% to 66.9%
   of total attribution, this is distributed across 7 channels. On a per-channel
   density basis, electricity history accounts for 76.2% to 88.4% of total attribution
   density. This explains why degrading the single electricity channel produces
   devastating forecast errors, while degrading weather produces minor disruption.
2. Dynamic Attribution Reallocation Under Missingness:
   When weather is masked (N-A), models dynamically reallocate gradient flow toward
   the intact electricity modality (+4.88% to +8.14% electricity share increase).
   Conversely, when electricity is masked (A-N), models reallocate attribution toward
   intact weather features (-7.71% to -8.79% electricity share drop).
3. Attribution Invariance Under Noise:
   Under 20% AWGN noise (A-A), attribution shift was virtually zero (<= 0.08 percentage
   points). This demonstrates that recurrent cell gradient flow is essentially
   unperturbed by zero-mean additive Gaussian perturbations.
4. Temporal Memory Concentration:
   In Late Fusion and Hybrid Fusion, electricity attribution was almost entirely (>99%)
   concentrated within the most recent 24 hours (Lags 0-23h). In contrast, Early
   Fusion distributed 24.9% of electricity attribution into intermediate lags (24-71h)
   and 2.8% into distant lags (72-167h), preserving weekly seasonality.
5. Weather Feature Hierarchy:
   Temperature ('temp') and apparent temperature ('feels_like') consistently ranked
   as the top two weather features, capturing 45% to 58% of all weather attribution.
   Wind direction sin/cos components captured the lowest attribution (<8% each).

Key Uncertainty Quantification Findings:
1. Constant Interval Width Property:
   Because primary conformal intervals use fixed clean-calibrated q_h thresholds,
   the Mean Prediction Interval Width (MPIW) remains strictly invariant across test
   degradation conditions (Early Fusion: 268.26 W; Late Fusion: 286.60 W; Hybrid
   Fusion: 284.26 W; Energy-Only: 267.13 W). Primary intervals DO NOT widen when test
   inputs degrade; instead, degradation manifests as empirical coverage erosion
   and interval score deterioration.
2. Clean Calibration Accuracy:
   Late Fusion achieved empirical coverage closest to the 90.0% nominal target
   (89.84% PICP, ACE = 0.16%), but required wider intervals (286.60 W). Early Fusion
   achieved 88.56% PICP (ACE = 1.44%) while achieving the sharpest interval score
   among multimodal models (360.65).
3. Coverage Breakdown Under Electricity Missingness:
   Under joint missingness (A-A), Energy-Only coverage collapsed to 79.92% (ACE = 10.08%,
   Winkler score surged to 399.39). Early Fusion dropped to 85.75% (ACE = 4.25%).
   Late Fusion exhibited the highest coverage resilience, maintaining 88.42% coverage
   (ACE = 1.58%) due to its wider baseline intervals.
4. Conformal Robustness Under Noise:
   Under 20% AWGN noise (A-A), empirical coverage remained completely stable across
   all models (Early Fusion: 88.58% vs 88.56% clean; Late Fusion: 89.79% vs 89.84%
   clean). Within-model noise comparisons yielded p_holm = 1.000 (Not Significant).
5. Oracle Required Width Analysis (Descriptive):
   To restore nominal 90.0% coverage under A-A missingness, oracle test calibration
   revealed that Energy-Only would require an interval expansion of +19.13% (+51.11 W),
   Early Fusion would require +9.82% (+26.35 W), Hybrid Fusion +5.35% (+15.21 W), and
   Late Fusion only +2.97% (+8.50 W).

7. CROSS-STEP SYNTHESIS & EVIDENCE CONNECTIONS
================================================================================
Synthesizing Steps 4 through 9 reveals three interconnected research insights:

Insight 1: The Accuracy-Robustness Tradeoff in Fusion Architecture Design
  - Early Fusion maximizes clean-data point forecasting accuracy (71.64 W MAE, lowest
    across all models) and yields the sharpest prediction intervals (360.65 Winkler score).
  - Late Fusion and Hybrid Fusion provide greater relative error buffering against
    sensor missingness (Hybrid degraded by only +2.95% relative MAE under A-A vs
    +6.64% for Early Fusion) and maintain higher empirical interval coverage (Late
    Fusion retained 88.42% PICP under A-A).
  - Crucially, Early Fusion's absolute performance under dual missingness (76.39 W MAE)
    remains significantly superior to Hybrid Fusion's clean performance (82.19 W MAE).
    Therefore, for real-world deployment, Early Fusion remains the preferred architecture
    despite experiencing higher relative degradation.

Insight 2: Modality Asymmetry is Grounded in Feature Attribution Density
  - The empirical finding that electricity missingness causes 10x greater error surges
    than weather missingness directly aligns with Step 8 Integrated Gradients:
    electricity carries 76.2% to 88.4% of single-channel attribution density.
  - In household load forecasting, weather variables provide subtle modulating context
    (thermodynamic heating/cooling demand), whereas past consumption reflects direct
    occupant behavioral routines. When past consumption is lost, weather cannot
    substitute for household behavioral state.

Insight 3: Zero-Mean Noise Invariance vs Zero-Imputation Bias
  - 20% additive Gaussian noise produced negligible forecasting degradation (<0.15 W MAE
    increase), zero statistical significance in multimodal models (p_holm = 1.0),
    zero attribution shift (<= 0.08% shift), and stable conformal coverage.
  - In contrast, 30% MCAR zero-masking caused large degradation because zero-imputation
    in standardized space replaces missing values with the training mean (307.17 W),
    systematically pulling peak load predictions downward toward the baseline mean.

================================================================================
8. METHODOLOGICAL LIMITATIONS & THREATS TO VALIDITY
================================================================================
1. Single Household Temporal Scope:
   All evaluations were performed on 587 rolling windows from a single residential
   meter. While providing high temporal granularity over multi-season transitions,
   findings characterize this specific dwelling and cannot be generalized
   unconditionally across different building archetypes, microclimates, or solar prosumers.
2. Sliding Window Autocorrelation:
   Consecutive 24-hour forecast windows overlap by 23 hours in the sliding-window
   protocol. While paired testing cancels shared environmental shocks and the HLN-DM
   test adjusts for multi-step serial correlation, the effective degrees of freedom
   is lower than N = 587 independent trials.
3. Fixed Degradation Protocols:
   The study strictly examined 30% MCAR missingness (mean-imputed) and 20% AWGN noise.
   Structural multi-hour contiguous outages, sensor drift, delayed telemetry, or
   extreme noise levels (eta > 0.50) were not evaluated.
4. Marginal vs Conditional Conformal Coverage:
   Split conformal prediction provides finite-sample marginal coverage guarantees
   under exchangeability. It does not provide formal conditional guarantees for
   specific hours or peak load regimes, and coverage naturally erodes when test
   inputs suffer distribution shift.
5. Local Gradient Explainability:
   Integrated Gradients measures local model sensitivity relative to a mean baseline;
   it establishes computational reliance within the network, not physical causality.

================================================================================
9. EVIDENCE-BASED RESEARCH CONCLUSIONS
================================================================================
1. Multimodal Early Fusion is the Optimal Architecture:
   Early Fusion achieved the lowest forecast error under clean operation (71.64 W MAE,
   p < 1e-16), retained the highest accuracy under dual sensor degradation (76.39 W MAE),
   and produced the sharpest prediction intervals (360.65 Winkler score).
2. Decoupled Fusion Fails to Justify Model Complexity:
   Late Fusion and Hybrid Fusion added over 50,000 parameters and doubled training
   complexity without improving clean forecasting accuracy over the unimodal baseline.
3. Household Load Forecasting is Inherently Asymmetric:
   Energy history is the dominant operational signal. Auxiliary weather data provides
   secondary contextual gains, but weather sensors cannot rescue forecasts when
   meter telemetry is corrupted.
4. Recurrent Neural Networks are Robust to Noise but Vulnerable to Missingness:
   Standard LSTM forecasting architectures naturally filter 20% measurement noise,
   but suffer severe performance collapse under structured missingness when using
   naive mean imputation.
5. Deployable Uncertainty Requires Adaptive Formulation:
   Fixed clean-calibrated conformal intervals maintain constant width and experience
   coverage drops under test missingness. Practical deployment requires either
   degradation-aware adaptive conformal intervals or multi-imputation pipelines.
