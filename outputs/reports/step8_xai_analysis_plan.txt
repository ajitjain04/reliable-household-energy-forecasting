1. CORE XAI OBJECTIVE & RESEARCH QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The purpose of Step 8 is to provide interpretability grounded directly in the
core project research question:

  "How does each fusion strategy rely on the electricity and weather modalities,
   and does this reliance change when one or both modalities become unreliable?"

Rather than generic feature-importance analysis, this investigation focuses on:
  A. Modality-level attribution: Quantifying the relative contribution of the
     electricity modality (historical power demand) versus the weather modality
     (7 atmospheric features) across the 168-hour history window.
  B. Attribution shift under degradation: Determining whether models reallocate
     reliance between modalities when weather (N–A), electricity (A–N), or both
     (A–A) are degraded by 30% MCAR missingness or 20% AWGN noise.
  C. Cross-architectural comparison: Contrasting the modality reliance profiles
     of Early Fusion (input concatenation), Late Fusion (independent encoders),
     and Hybrid Fusion (intermediate sequence fusion + joint temporal LSTM).
  D. Linking attribution to empirical robustness: Investigating whether the
     empirical degradation patterns discovered in Steps 5, 6, and 7 (e.g. large
     electricity sensitivity vs minimal weather sensitivity) correspond to
     the models' internal attribution distributions under clean conditions.


2. MODELS TO ANALYZE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Four models are included in the experimental scope:

1. Energy-Only (Step 4.1):
   - Unimodal baseline receiving strictly electricity history (1 feature).
   - Contains zero weather features.
   - Role in XAI: Serves as a reference control for the temporal attribution
     profile of electricity demand and establishes the baseline attribution
     scale. It is NOT analyzed as a two-modality model.

2. Early Fusion (Step 4.2):
   - Multimodal model fusing energy and weather at the raw input layer.
   - Evaluated for 2-modality attribution and degradation reallocation.

3. Late Fusion (Step 4.3):
   - Multimodal model with separate modality encoders fused at the latent head.
   - Evaluated for 2-modality attribution and encoder isolation effects.

4. Hybrid Fusion (Step 4.4):
   - Multimodal model with separate encoders, intermediate timestep-level
     fusion, and a joint temporal LSTM.
   - Evaluated for 2-modality attribution across intermediate sequence layers.


3. RELIABILITY CONDITIONS TO EVALUATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Each of the three multimodal models will be evaluated across 7 operational regimes:

1. Clean Reference:
   - N–N: Normal electricity + normal weather (Clean benchmark)

2. Step 5A — 30% MCAR Missingness Regimes:
   - N–A: Normal electricity + 30% missing weather (imputed with 0.0)
   - A–N: 30% missing electricity + normal weather
   - A–A: 30% missing electricity + 30% missing weather

3. Step 5B — 20% Additive Gaussian Noise Regimes:
   - N–A: Normal electricity + 20% noisy weather (AWGN, eta = 0.20)
   - A–N: 20% noisy electricity + normal weather
   - A–A: 20% noisy electricity + 20% noisy weather

The missingness and noise degradation mechanisms are kept strictly separate;
they are never combined into a single condition.


4. ARCHITECTURE AUDIT OF FROZEN CHECKPOINTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A direct code and checkpoint audit was conducted across the source files:

A. Early Fusion (src/models/early_fusion.py):
   - Checkpoint: outputs/models/early_fusion/best_model.pt (parameter count: 10 tensors)
   - Input structure: Single tensor of shape (batch, 168, 8).
     * Index 0: hourly_active_power (Electricity modality)
     * Indices 1..7: temp, feels_like, humidity, pressure, speed, wind_sin, wind_cos (Weather modality)
   - Temporal layers: 2-layer LSTM(input_size=8, hidden_size=64, dropout=0.2, batch_first=True).
   - Fusion: None inside the network. Concatenation occurs at the input dataset level.
   - Output projection: Takes last timestep hidden state out[:, -1, :] (batch, 64) -> Linear(64, 24).

B. Late Fusion (src/models/late_fusion.py):
   - Checkpoint: outputs/models/late_fusion/best_model.pt (parameter count: 20 tensors)
   - Input structure: Dual tensors passed to forward(energy, weather):
     * energy: (batch, 168, 1)
     * weather: (batch, 168, 7)
   - Modality encoders:
     * energy_encoder: 2-layer LSTM(1, 64, dropout=0.2) -> last hidden rep_e (batch, 64)
     * weather_encoder: 2-layer LSTM(7, 64, dropout=0.2) -> last hidden rep_w (batch, 64)
   - Fusion layer: Concatenates representations [rep_e, rep_w] (batch, 128) -> Linear(128, 64) + ReLU.
   - Output projection: Linear(64, 24) applied to the fused latent representation.

C. Hybrid Fusion (src/models/hybrid_fusion.py):
   - Checkpoint: outputs/models/hybrid_fusion/best_model.pt (parameter count: 24 tensors)
   - Input structure: Dual tensors passed to forward(energy, weather):
     * energy: (batch, 168, 1)
     * weather: (batch, 168, 7)
   - Modality encoders:
     * energy_encoder: 2-layer LSTM(1, 64) -> keeps full sequence out_e (batch, 168, 64)
     * weather_encoder: 2-layer LSTM(7, 64) -> keeps full sequence out_w (batch, 168, 64)
   - Intermediate fusion: Concatenates sequences along feature axis (batch, 168, 128)
     -> Linear(128, 64) + ReLU applied across all 168 timesteps -> (batch, 168, 64).
   - Joint temporal modeling: 1-layer LSTM(64, 64) over the fused sequence -> takes last state (batch, 64).
   - Output projection: Linear(64, 24) applied to joint representation.


5. XAI METHOD SELECTION & JUSTIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We evaluated four candidate attribution paradigms:

A. Candidate Methods:
   1. Integrated Gradients (Sundararajan et al., 2017):
      - Axiomatically justified (satisfies Completeness, Implementation Invariance,
        Linearity, and Sensitivity).
      - Integrates gradients along the straight-line path from a baseline x' to input x:
          IG_i(x) = (x_i - x'_i) * integral_0^1 [ dF(x' + alpha*(x - x')) / dx_i ] d_alpha
      - Avoids gradient saturation common in deep recurrent networks.
      - Provides full temporal resolution: attribution tensor of shape (168, F) for each sample.

   2. Gradient x Input (First-order Taylor approximation):
      - Simple and fast, but can saturate and lacks completeness.

   3. Modality Occlusion / Feature Ablation:
      - Perturbation-based counterfactual: measures Delta F when a modality is zeroed out.
      - Useful at the coarse modality level, but computationally expensive for 168 temporal lags.

   4. SHAP (DeepSHAP / KernelSHAP):
      - DeepSHAP suffers from PyTorch LSTM op compatibility issues.
      - KernelSHAP is model-agnostic but requires thousands of permutations per sample,
        making 587 sequence windows computationally intractable on CPU.

B. Method Selection Decision:
   - PRIMARY METHOD: INTEGRATED GRADIENTS (IG) with m = 20 Riemann summation steps.
   - VERIFICATION / SECONDARY CHECKS:
     * Gradient x Input: Computed to verify linear consistency.
     * Modality Occlusion: Evaluated at the coarse level to verify modality share rankings.


6. BASELINE / REFERENCE SELECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The baseline x' is the foundational reference against which attributions are measured.

Selected Baseline:
  x' = 0.0 across all features and timesteps in standardised / scaled space.

Justification:
  1. Statistical Grounding: All features were preprocessed with standardisation
     (mean = 0, std = 1). A zero vector represents the empirical training mean of
     household active power (307.17 W) and all weather variables (e.g. 20.8°C temp).
  2. Protocol Alignment: In the Step 5A missingness experiment, missing values were
     explicitly imputed with 0.0 in standardised space. Using x' = 0.0 aligns the
     XAI baseline directly with the uninformative state of the degradation protocol.
  3. Interpretability: Deviations (x - x') represent deviations from average consumption
     and average weather conditions.


7. ATTRIBUTION TARGET SPECIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The forecasting models predict a 24-dimensional vector: \hat{y} = [\hat{y}_1, ..., \hat{y}_{24}].

Selected Primary Target:
  Aggregate 24-Hour Forecast Output:
    S(\hat{y}) = sum_{h=1}^{24} \hat{y}_h

Justification:
  1. Represents total daily forecasted electricity consumption in scaled space.
  2. Avoids arbitrary single-horizon cherry-picking.
  3. Mathematical property: Due to the linearity of gradients:
       d S(\hat{y}) / dx = sum_{h=1}^{24} [ d \hat{y}_h / dx ]
     Attribution of the aggregate sum equals the exact sum of attributions across
     all 24 individual forecast horizons.

Supplementary Horizon-Specific Targets:
  To investigate whether weather reliance increases at longer forecast horizons,
  attributions will be derived separately for selected representative lead times:
    - h = 1  (Immediate 1-hour ahead forecast)
    - h = 6  (Short-term morning transition)
    - h = 12 (Mid-day forecast)
    - h = 24 (Full-day ahead forecast)


8. MODALITY AGGREGATION & NORMALIZATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For sample i, IG produces attribution tensor A(i, t, f) of shape (168, F).

A. Modality Sums:
   - Electricity magnitude:
       |A_energy(i)| = sum_{t=1}^{168} |A(i, t, 0)|
   - Weather magnitude:
       |A_weather(i)| = sum_{t=1}^{168} sum_{f=1}^{7} |A(i, t, f)|

B. Metric 1: Total Modality Attribution Share:
   - Reflects total information flow into the model:
       Share_energy(i)  = |A_energy(i)|  / ( |A_energy(i)| + |A_weather(i)| )
       Share_weather(i) = |A_weather(i)| / ( |A_energy(i)| + |A_weather(i)| )
   - Verified property: Share_energy + Share_weather = 1.0 (100%).

C. Metric 2: Dimensionality-Normalized Attribution Density:
   - Accounts for the feature asymmetry (1 energy feature vs 7 weather features):
       Density_energy(i)  = |A_energy(i)|  / (1 * 168)
       Density_weather(i) = |A_weather(i)| / (7 * 168)
       Density_Share_energy(i)  = Density_energy(i)  / ( Density_energy(i) + Density_weather(i) )
       Density_Share_weather(i) = Density_weather(i) / ( Density_energy(i) + Density_weather(i) )
   - Measures attribution per feature channel.


9. TEMPORAL DIMENSION & LAG AGGREGATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The 168 historical hours (7 days) will be analyzed across two views:

A. Full Continuous Lag Curve:
   - For each lag t in {1, ..., 168} (where t=168 is the most recent observation t-0,
     and t=1 is 7 days prior t-167):
       T_energy(t)  = (1 / N) * sum_{i=1}^N |A_energy(i, t)|
       T_weather(t) = (1 / N) * sum_{i=1}^N sum_{f=1}^7 |A_weather(i, t, f)|

B. Standardized Temporal Bins:
   - Recent History (0–23h lag / Day -1): Timesteps t in [145, 168] (past 24h).
   - Intermediate History (24–71h lag / Days -2 to -3): Timesteps t in [97, 144] (prior 48h).
   - Distant History (72–167h lag / Days -4 to -7): Timesteps t in [1, 96] (prior 96h).
   - This determines whether recurrent states decay historical memory or preserve
     weekly periodic patterns.


10. WEATHER FEATURE ATTRIBUTION BREAKDOWN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The 7 causal weather features will be analyzed individually:
  1. temp
  2. feels_like
  3. humidity
  4. pressure
  5. speed
  6. wind_sin
  7. wind_cos

Features explicitly excluded:
  - Non-causal or leak-prone features (temp_t+1, feels_like_t+1, temp_min, temp_max,
    text descriptions).

Metrics per weather feature:
  - Mean absolute attribution: (1 / N) * sum_i sum_t |A(i, t, f)|
  - Within-weather percentage share: sum_t |A(t, f)| / sum_{f'} sum_t |A(t, f')|
  - Feature ranking across models and conditions.


11. CONDITION COMPARISON & ATTRIBUTION SHIFT PROTOCOL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To test whether models reallocate reliance when inputs degrade:

For each model M and condition C in {NA, AN, AA}:
  - Delta Share_energy(M, C)  = Share_energy(M, C)  - Share_energy(M, NN)
  - Delta Share_weather(M, C) = Share_weather(M, C) - Share_weather(M, NN)

Key Research Hypotheses to Test:
  1. When weather is degraded (N–A), does weather attribution drop and shift toward energy?
  2. When electricity is degraded (A–N), does electricity attribution drop and shift toward weather?
  3. When both are degraded (A–A), does the relative share reflect the clean distribution or a collapsed state?

Neutral Reporting Language:
  Use "attribution share shifted by Delta percentage points", avoiding claims of
  "active cognitive adaptation" unless architectural mechanisms explicitly demonstrate it.


12. LINKING XAI TO EMPIRICAL RELIABILITY FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
XAI results will be directly cross-referenced against Steps 5, 6, and 7:

  - Finding in Step 7: Electricity missingness degraded MAE by +1.89 W to +9.13 W
    (p < 1e-15), while weather missingness had minimal impact (<= +0.65 W, and p = 0.5604
    for Early Fusion).
  - Hypothesis: Clean models exhibit overwhelming electricity attribution dominance
    (Share_energy >> Share_weather).
  - Robustness ranking comparison:
    Hybrid Fusion showed the lowest relative degradation (+2.95% vs +6.64% for Early Fusion).
    Does Hybrid Fusion exhibit a fundamentally different modality attribution balance
    due to its separate encoders and intermediate fusion layer?

16. METHODOLOGICAL LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Attribution is model-dependent and reflects internal feature weighting, not causal reality.
2. Baseline sensitivity: Results are conditioned on the zero-mean baseline (x' = 0).
3. Collinearity: Weather variables (e.g. temp and feels_like) are correlated, which can
   distribute attribution across redundant channels.
4. Feature dimensionality asymmetry: 1 energy feature vs 7 weather features. Reporting
   both total share and normalized density is required to prevent misleading claims.
5. Single household dataset: Findings characterize this specific residential profile.
