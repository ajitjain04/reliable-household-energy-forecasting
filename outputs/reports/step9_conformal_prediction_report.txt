1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & PROTOCOL OVERVIEW
================================================================================
This report presents the complete empirical results of Step 9: Conformal Prediction
and Uncertainty Quantification for multi-horizon household energy forecasting.
The experiment rigorously evaluates whether prediction intervals calibrated under nominal
clean conditions remain reliable and well-calibrated when input modalities undergo realistic
degradations (30% MCAR missingness and 20% additive Gaussian noise).

Protocol Specifications:
  - Calibration Method: Horizon-Wise Absolute-Residual Split Conformal Prediction.
  - Significance Level: alpha = 0.10 -> Target Nominal Coverage = 90.0%.
  - Calibration Sample Size: N_cal = 587 clean rolling windows (untouched calibration set).
  - Conformal Order Statistic: k = ceil((587 + 1) * 0.90) = 530 (530th smallest residual).
  - Calibration Policy: Calibrate ONCE on clean N-N data; freeze 24 horizon-wise quantiles (q_h);
    apply fixed quantiles across all 7 operational regimes (28 total evaluations).
  - Prediction Intervals: Raw symmetric intervals [L_i,h = yhat_i,h - q_h, U_i,h = yhat_i,h + q_h].
  - Key Metric: Winkler Interval Score (Interval Score with alpha=0.10, penalty factor 20.0).

5. KEY RESEARCH FINDINGS & RELIABILITY SYNTHESIS
================================================================================
1. CLEAN NOMINAL CALIBRATION QUALITY:
   - Late Fusion achieved the highest clean empirical coverage at 89.84% (ACE = 0.16%),
     remarkably close to the 90.0% nominal target.
   - Early Fusion achieved 88.56% coverage (ACE = 1.44%) while maintaining the sharpest
     interval score among multimodal models (360.65 vs 382.96 for Late Fusion).
   - Energy-Only baseline achieved 86.39% coverage (ACE = 3.61%), reflecting higher error dispersion.
   - Hybrid Fusion achieved 88.47% coverage (ACE = 1.53%, Interval Score = 380.79).

2. ASYMMETRIC SENSITIVITY: ELECTRICITY VS WEATHER MISSINGNESS:
   - Weather missingness (N-A) produced negligible coverage deterioration across all architectures:
     * Early Fusion: 88.86% coverage (vs 88.56% clean)
     * Late Fusion: 89.79% coverage (vs 89.84% clean)
     * Hybrid Fusion: 88.25% coverage (vs 88.47% clean)
   - Electricity missingness (A-N and A-A) caused severe coverage breakdown:
     * Energy-Only coverage collapsed to 79.92% (ACE = 10.08%, Interval Score surged to 399.39).
     * Early Fusion coverage dropped to 85.75% under A-A (ACE = 4.25%).
     * Late Fusion dropped to 88.42% under A-A (ACE = 1.58%).
     * Hybrid Fusion dropped to 86.84% under A-A (ACE = 3.16%).
   - Statistical significance: Clean vs Missingness A-N and A-A differences are statistically
     significant across all models (p_holm < 0.001).

3. MISSINGNESS VS MEASUREMENT NOISE:
   - 20% Additive Gaussian Noise (AWGN, eta = 0.20) caused virtually NO coverage or interval score
     degradation across any model:
     * Early Fusion coverage remained 88.58% under A-A noise (Mean IS = 360.62 vs 360.65 clean).
     * Late Fusion coverage remained 89.79% under A-A noise (Mean IS = 383.01 vs 382.96 clean).
     * Hybrid Fusion coverage remained 88.41% under A-A noise (Mean IS = 380.74 vs 380.79 clean).
   - Within multimodal architectures, within-model noise comparisons (Clean vs Noise) were
     statistically non-significant after Holm-Bonferroni correction (p_holm > 0.05).
   - The observed results indicate that the tested 20% AWGN condition had substantially less effect on uncertainty calibration than the tested 30% MCAR missingness condition, consistent with the corresponding forecasting-reliability results from Steps 5 and 6.

4. ARCHITECTURAL TRADE-OFFS & CONNECTIONS TO STEPS 5-8:
   - Early Fusion achieves both superior forecasting accuracy (lowest MAE) and sharpest
     nominal intervals (lowest Interval Score: 360.65).
   - Late Fusion provides wider intervals (MPIW = 286.60 W vs 268.26 W for Early Fusion),
     giving it greater coverage resilience against power missingness (88.42% vs 85.75%).
   - Hybrid Fusion exhibits balanced performance (MPIW = 284.26 W), maintaining 86.84% coverage
     under dual missingness.
   - Alignment with XAI (Step 8): This is consistent with the Step 8 Integrated Gradients analysis, which showed substantially higher per-channel attribution density for the electricity modality than for weather across the multimodal architectures. Consequently, degradation of the electricity history produced larger uncertainty deterioration than weather degradation under the tested missingness protocol.

6. SECONDARY DESCRIPTIVE ANALYSES
================================================================================
A. NON-NEGATIVE PHYSICAL CLIPPING [max(0, L_i,h), U_i,h]:
   - Under the project's non-negative active power target assumption (y >= 0), clipping the lower
     bound to zero [max(0, L_i,h), U_i,h] mathematically preserves empirical coverage because for any
     y >= 0, the event {y >= max(0, L)} is logically equivalent to {y >= L} when the upper bound
     U_i,h remains unchanged (PICP remains identical).
   - In live inference and reporting, clipping reduces effective interval width (e.g., from 268.26 W
     to 267.42 W for Early Fusion) and provides physically plausible non-negative load bounds.

B. ORACLE TEST-SET REQUIRED INTERVAL WIDTH (DESCRIPTIVE ONLY):
   - To achieve >= 90.0% empirical coverage under test missingness A-A, the required oracle MPIW would be:
     * Energy-Only: 326.85 W (+59.72 W increase over clean 267.13 W)

     * Late Fusion: 297.82 W (+11.22 W increase over clean 286.60 W)

   - This descriptive metric demonstrates that Early Fusion would require only a 10.1% width
     expansion to restore nominal coverage, whereas Energy-Only requires a 22.4% expansion.

7. METHODOLOGICAL LIMITATIONS
================================================================================
1. Rolling-Window Dependence & Boundary Overlap:
   - Sequences advance by a one-hour stride, causing adjacent 192-hour windows (168h history + 24h horizon)
     to share 167 history hours and 23 target hours.
   - At the partition boundary, the Step 3.7 split report explicitly records:
     "Test history overlaps Cal target: True" (Test min history 2023-12-01 15:00 vs Cal max target 2023-12-09 13:00).
   - Chronological forecast-origin separation does not establish exchangeability of dependent rolling windows.
     Consequently, standard split-conformal i.i.d./exchangeability assumptions are violated, and no formal
     time-series conformal coverage guarantee is established.
2. Nominal vs Empirical Coverage:
   - The 90.0% figure represents nominal calibration coverage.
   - Observed coverage under temporal autocorrelation, rolling dependence, or data degradation is strictly
     empirical and is not guaranteed to equal 90.0% out-of-distribution (e.g., observed coverage drops to
     85.75% under severe dual missingness and 79.92% for Energy-Only).
3. Marginal vs Conditional Coverage:
   - Split conformal prediction targets marginal coverage across the benchmark population; it does not
     guarantee 90% conditional coverage for individual hours, load regimes, or specific meteorological states.
